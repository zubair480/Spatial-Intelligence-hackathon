# Demo script — 2:00, read aloud

Read at roughly 165 words per minute. Bracketed italics are stage directions,
not spoken. Everything else is spoken exactly as written. Practice the 1:15–1:40
block twice — it is the densest 25 seconds in the script and the easiest to rush.

---

## 0:00–0:15 — Problem, track, loop

*[Both browser windows already open, side by side, before you start talking.]*

Co-op games put two players in one world at the same moment.

For Gaming and Interactive Worlds, we put them in the same house twenty-eight
years apart.

One player acts in 1998, the other watches 2026 rewrite itself around her, and
all they share is a phone line.

---

## 0:15–1:15 — The demo

*[No implementation talk in this block. Narrate what is on screen, nothing else.]*

*[Gesture across both windows.]*

Two players, two windows, one screen, so you can watch cause and effect at the
same time. On the left, a kitchen in 1998. On the right, the same kitchen in
2026. All they share is a phone line.

*[1998 player picks up the brass key.]*

He finds a brass key on the counter. Clean, new, 1998.

*[2026 player pans to the wall beside the fridge.]*

She's locked in, there's a timer running, and she's looking at flat paint. She
tells him there should be a cavity behind the radiator.

*[1998 player opens the access panel.]*

He opens the access panel that still exists in his kitchen.

*[Beat. Do not talk over this.]*

Watch her wall. A seam comes up through twenty-eight years of paint while she is
standing in front of it.

*[1998 player drops the key into the cavity.]*

He drops the key in.

*[Beat. Let the 2026 window land on its own.]*

Twenty-eight years of corrosion happen to a key she has never seen. From where
she's standing it has been in that wall her entire life.

*[Twist card fires on the 1998 window. Point at it.]*

And now he gets a card she will never see. He's the one this house happened to.
He can nail that panel shut, seal the key inside the wall, and lie to her on the
phone, and she can only ever see consequences, never actions. She has no way to
know.

*[2026 player takes the key, unlocks the door.]*

This time he let her out.

---

## 1:15–1:40 — Sponsor technologies

*[One sentence each. Do not elaborate. Keep clicking through the stack slide.]*

Convex: the time machine is a database transaction — one write in 1998 patches
every derived 2026 row, so her world changes mid-sentence.

World Labs: Marble generated the house itself, the same kitchen in both eras,
as navigable worlds.

Tripo: every object that crosses time is a Tripo model generated twice — new in
1998, weathered by twenty-eight years.

Mint: Mint's MCP and Three.js skills assembled the scene layer and generated the
phone audio and room tone.

---

## 1:40–2:00 — Close

Two clients, two timelines, one shared causal state, generated worlds.

Keep Talking and Nobody Explodes was a game-jam project, and its creators quit
their jobs to ship it.

That engine also runs escape-room design, location-based entertainment, and
multiplayer procedural training.

*[Stop. Do not add a thank-you. Let the two windows sit there.]*
